# 02 — Kid Interview Kit (Mom Test–coded)

**Goal:** verbatim signals from 3 Roblox-aged kids (9-15) on whether they'd play Founder Mode and why. The Mom Test rule: never ask "would you play this?" (people lie). Ask about specific past behavior. Listen for *specific*, not *generic*.

**Critical:** these MUST be kids 9-15, NOT adults from your peer network. The eng review locked this. Spaza/stokvel/taxi rank is your generation's lived experience, not necessarily theirs.

---

## Who to text

Cousins, nieces/nephews, kids of close friends, neighbours' kids (with parental approval). Pick three different *types* of kids if possible:

- **Kid A:** A heavy Roblox player. Knows Brookhaven, Adopt Me, Bloxburg, Mzansi Style. Validates discoverability and genre fit.
- **Kid B:** A kid with kasi cultural fluency (raised in or visiting townships regularly, family runs a stokvel/spaza). Validates cultural authenticity.
- **Kid C:** A kid with neither (e.g., a suburban kid who plays Roblox occasionally, or a kasi-rooted kid who plays Fortnite more than Roblox). Validates whether the wedge works *outside* the obvious sweet spot.

If you can only get 3 of the same type, that's still data — note it in the capture template.

---

## Permission protocol (mandatory)

For any kid under 16, get explicit permission from a parent/guardian before sending the video or asking questions. Frame it as: *"I'm thinking about building a game and want my niece's honest opinion. 5-min chat, no recording. OK?"* Most parents will say yes. Document the permission in the capture template.

---

## The 4-question script (use verbatim — don't improvise)

**Opening (do not skip):**

> "Hey — I'm working on something and I want your honest take. There are no right answers, and 'this is dumb' is the most useful thing you could say. I'm going to show you a 30-second video, then ask four quick questions. Cool?"

Send the 30-second cut from the concept video. Wait for them to watch.

**Question 1 — Past behavior, not future intent (Mom Test core):**

> "What's the last Roblox game you played for more than 30 minutes in one sitting? What was good about it? Why'd you keep playing?"

**Why this question:** Reveals what genres and mechanics actually hold them. If they say "Adopt Me because of trades," tycoons might not land. If they say "Bloxburg because I built a house," tycoons might land hard. Listen for the *because*.

---

**Question 2 — Recognition test:**

> "After watching that video, what did you think the game was about? Don't read the tagline — what did YOU see?"

**Why this question:** If they say "spaza shop game" — your visuals landed. If they say "I don't know" — the video failed at the recognition layer. If they say "tycoon" — even better, that's the Roblox-genre signal. If they say "education game" — the stealth pedagogy failed (start over).

---

**Question 3 — Specific demand probe:**

> "If this was on Roblox right now, what would make you click play? And what would make you close it after 60 seconds?"

**Why this question:** Specific click-triggers and bounce-triggers. Listen for *specific UI elements* (the icon, the title, the description copy). Listen for *specific friction* (loading time, mobile fit, "I don't get it"). This is the most actionable answer of the four.

---

**Question 4 — Friend test (the real signal):**

> "Would you send the video to a friend right now? If yes, who? If no, why not?"

**Why this question:** This is the strongest demand signal in the kit. "Yes I'd send it to [name] because she's into kasi style" = real demand. "No, it's cringe" = the brand doesn't land. "No, I don't know what to say about it" = the video failed at clarity. If they NAME a specific friend, you've found a viral seed; ask if they'll actually send it.

## After all 3 interviews — synthesis (do this within 24h)

Make a fourth file: `synthesis-2026-05-XX.md`. Answer these:

1. **Genre fit:** Did 2+ of 3 mention tycoon/sim/build mechanics in Q1 as their "kept playing because"? (yes = tycoon genre is right) (no = mechanic mismatch)
2. **Recognition:** Did 2+ of 3 read the video correctly in Q2 (spaza/tycoon/business)? (yes = video lands) (no = re-cut or rewrite)
3. **Cringe check:** Did any of 3 use "cringe," "embarrassing," "trying too hard," "edutainment-y"? (any one = brand-tone problem)
4. **Friend test:** Did 2+ of 3 name a specific friend they'd send it to? (yes = viral seed exists) (no = niche too narrow OR cringe-coded)
5. **Cultural authenticity:** Did the kasi-fluent kid (B) approve cultural details, or flag anything as off?

**Decision rule:** if Q1 + Q2 + Q4 all hit, GREEN LIGHT v1 build. If only 1-2 hit, sharpen the wedge before code (likely candidates: tighten the video, swap the brand color, simplify the mechanic).

---

## Pitfalls

- **Don't pitch.** You're not selling, you're listening. If you find yourself explaining what the game IS after Q2, you're talking too much. Their wrong reading is the data.
- **Don't take "I'd play it" as a green light.** Politeness inflates intent. The Q4 friend test is the only believable demand signal.
- **Don't lead with "I made this."** Frame: "I'm thinking about this." Lower stakes = more honest answers.
- **Don't ask leading questions.** "Doesn't this look fun?" → useless. "What did you see?" → useful.
- **Don't aggregate too fast.** Three datapoints, but each verbatim quote matters more than the summary stats. Re-read individual transcripts before drawing patterns.
